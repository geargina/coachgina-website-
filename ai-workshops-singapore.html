How a session runs

Example of a workshop run. Subject to change depending on the AI maturity of the class.

15 min before

Doors and login triage

Auth is the number one time thief in a hands-on room. Doors open early purely so anyone staring at a red screen gets fixed before the clock starts.

0:00 to 0:10

Welcome and the level check

90 seconds on who is teaching, then hands up. 3 questions, and I watch the drop between the second and the third, then say it out loud. The gap between paying for a tool and getting value back from it is the whole reason the room is here.

0:10 to 0:30

Foundations, fast

What AI is, how it learned, a 60-second history, and the car wash test where a thinking model still walks through the car wash. The point I want in their heads before anything else: AI follows patterns, not logic, and your context is what turns those patterns into reasoning.

0:30 to 0:55

Chat, properly

Which model to reach for, the effort dial, and how to brief one like a new hire on their first day. Then the same question asked twice live, cold first and briefed second. Nobody argues with the difference once they have watched it. Hallucination gets said plainly here, not buried.

0:55 to 1:12

Connectors and the safety question

Chat gives the assistant a brain, connectors give it hands. A 2 minute inbox triage demo, then the answer to the question every room asks. Your login, deliberately narrow access, Drive read-only and Gmail read and draft. It reads and it drafts. It does not send, because I am the one who presses send.

1:12 to 1:55

Projects, then 30 min of building

Chat starts cold, a Project answers warm. I build one live on a real task, including the moment it refuses to invent experience that is not in the file. Then the room builds their own for 30 min. This is the biggest block on the sheet and it never gets cut.

1:55 to 2:05

Break

10 min. The openers slide stays up so anyone who wants to keep typing can.

2:05 to 2:28

Skills, then 15 min of building

Teach it once and it sticks. We scroll a real skill file, no code anywhere in it, then I change one line live and the behaviour changes with it. Then everyone writes their first one, usually by asking Claude to draft it, which is the fastest route for a beginner room.

2:28 to 2:50

Agentic work, then 15 min of delegating

Chat answers, an agent finishes. One demo of a job running end to end, narrated while it ticks, then everyone hands over a real task of their own. The habit I want installed is files plus outcome, not steps, and then reviewing the result like it came from a new hire.

2:50 to 3:00

Show and tell, and the 30-day path

2 or 3 volunteers, one minute each, picked because I saw them make something interesting during a build block. Then what to do in week one, week two, week three and at day 30, and the follow-up email with the prompts and the checklist.

What I ask people to bring

Sent to every participant 3 days before the session, in these words.

  • An account on the tool we are using, signed up and logged in before you arrive.
  • Your laptop and your charger.
  • One real piece of work. A report you write monthly, a deck you are building, a process doc. If you are between roles, bring your resume and one job ad you are eyeing instead.
  • Know your Google password. Connecting your tools happens live and the login is yours to do.

Questions before you book

How do I scope an AI workshop for my team?

Start with who is in the room. Group size, seniority, the tools they already pay for, how much AI they are in contact with daily, and the industry context that makes a generic example fall flat. Then the outcome. What should these people be doing differently next Monday. Send that on the contact form and you get a format and a shape back.

Do participants need any technical background?

None at all! Most of my rooms are people who have never opened an AI tool. Every technical term gets a plain English explanation the first time it comes up. You just need a laptop, an account logged in before we start, and one real piece of your own work. A report you write every month is perfect.

Can the workshop run online?

Yes! I've run online sessions with people joining from different regions. Online suits a talk or a 2 hr session well. For a full day, in person wins, because the build blocks depend on being able to look over shoulders.

How much notice do you need?

It depends on the scope. Off the shelf programmes can be done in a matter of days. Custom ones take 1 to 2 scoping calls, and the customising takes a couple of weeks to pre-plan.
